# AI-Native Product Marketing Maturity Model

**Version 1.0.3 — 2026-10-17.** Eleven dimensions, five levels (A–E). D1–D3 inherited by reference from STD-SHARED-INTELLIGENCE v1.2 (SDLC release v1.4.1); D10 inherited by reference from STD-EA-MM D10 at v1.1.2 (model text v1.1.1). The inherited text is rendered here from the sources at their tags. See `CHANGELOG.md`.

## Family maturity names

A — Nascent · B — Modeled · C — Continuous · D — Integral · E — Telemetric

Names, descriptions and the general shape: family_maturity_levels.md in github.com/superdtf-0882/ai-native-sdlc-maturity-model at v1.4.2.

## How to read this model

- D1–D3 and D10 are inherited by reference: D1–D3 from STD-SHARED-INTELLIGENCE v1.2, D10 from STD-EA-MM's Shared language. Their text is rendered here from the sources at their tags and is edited only there. D10 carries one vantage paragraph at E, which is this model's; D1–D3's are the standard's.

- Product Marketing-specific readings on top of the shared D1–D3 text: D1, no delta beyond the shared text. D2, from this chair the function's asset is usually the buyer persona. D3, the monitoring feed is often this function's; the positioning thesis is not.

- D10 is inherited rather than redrafted for the same reason D1–D3 are: the vocabulary is one enterprise asset, and a Product Marketing-owned vocabulary would be the separate departmental truth this model refuses. D10 owns the operation (the challenge at authorship, the resolution from context, the recorded collapse at the boundary). D5 owns what persists across assets (the claim, its approved wording, its provenance, the collapse record each asset carries). D4 owns standing (what a record is, where it came from, whether it is in force). D11 owns whether an actor may change, issue, or publish any of them.

- Assessors score the vocabulary where it actually lives. D10 is written for the enterprise; where no enterprise practice owns the vocabulary, Product Marketing is often its de facto owner, and the score is of that vocabulary as operated. The ownership question is raised as the organization matures, not settled by the model.

- D9 measures learning and correction from market response. D4 measures corpus integrity. D5 measures whether messaging, claims, and market-facing wording are coherent and substantiated. A claim can be well stored (D4), properly approved (D11), and correctly worded (D10) and still be unsupported; that is D5's signal. The three boundaries are read together.

- D5 at C checks claims against evidence and wording against the framework at authorship, where D10 at C challenges terms. These may be one surface. Two working checks are not less mature than one; the model describes what the checks do, not how many there are.

- Shared D1–D3 generate function-specific renderings at E and keep rendering manual at D. D6 and D8 surface current content selected by deal or adoption context at D; that is retrieval from the shared source, not a rendering generated for an audience, which is why it sits at D and not E. D5–D8 measure the quality and operation of their functional outputs, not whether a rendering can be generated.

- Modeled (B) in this family means four things at once: a deliberate method exists, it is manual, it is owned by one function, and the AI-assisted part of it follows a consistent method. The B cells of D4–D9 and D11 carry all four so that an AI-native B can be told from a pre-AI one. D10's B is the source model's.

- D9 is scored on how response evidence is gathered, linked, routed, and acted on, not on how fast the market answers. In calendar-bound markets such as K-12 districts, signal arrives on budget and school-year cycles; market-imposed signal latency is not itself a maturity deficit, and D9-E's "where signals permit" is read in that light.

- D11 covers decision rights and enforcement, not the entire security programme or enterprise resource allocation. Compare STD-SDLC-MM D11 and STD-PDLC-MM D6.

- Dimension order is presentation order, not a process. Dimensions are scored independently; ordinal levels are not averaged.

**Pre-AI and Exempt:** STD-EA-MM's text at v1.1.2, github.com/superdtf-0882/ai-native-ea-maturity-model.

- Status labels: Inherited · by reference means the text is the source's at the cited tag and is edited only there; Native means this model's own. Provenance is in each dimension's basis line, not in its status.

- Every level A–D carries a transition to the next level and a verification. The transition says what to change; the verification names observable evidence of the destination capability that a reader can establish without asking the author. Every E level carries sustainment: how to preserve the capability and detect drift, rather than a transition to a further level.

- A verification tests evidence, not craft. None asks whether the work was done well; each asks whether a stated condition holds and can be shown. Where a transition depends on another dimension — D8's step to E on D11 at D — the transition says so, which is the family's "what has to be true first."

- Score the normal operating capability against the full level definition. An isolated demonstration does not establish a standing practice. Where a verification depends on a condition that has not occurred during the review period, use a relevant historical case or a recorded controlled end-to-end exercise, alongside evidence that the capability operates routinely. The exercise must show the applicable records, authority checks, actions, and propagation; configuration alone is insufficient. Keep controlled evidence identified as such and separate from actual market outcomes. There is no need to manufacture a harmful incident or change real authority merely to obtain evidence.

- Positioning, pricing, packaging, and release goals pertain to the Product Management function regardless of the role, department, or actor performing the work. Messaging pertains to the Product Marketing function, which translates and operationalizes that direction. Participation in shared intelligence does not create a separate departmental truth.

- D6 measures whether the field can use current knowledge at the moment of a commercial conversation, not the quality of the content itself, which is D5. Sales owns commercial commitment; enablement never sets price or terms. That boundary holds at every level, so it is stated here and not in a level cell.

- D7 measures readiness and market introduction against release intent. Release-goal authority stays with Product Management at every level; the readiness record informs the release decision and never makes it. D7 at E aligns with STD-PDLC-MM D9 at E and STD-SDLC-MM D10 at E (progressive delivery).

- D8 governs outbound guidance that supports adoption; D9 governs what market and customer response teaches. Customer Success owns realized customer outcomes at every level. D8 at E depends on D11 at D, since agents send within delegated authority; the cell cites it in the family's form; see STD-SDLC-MM D10 at D.

### Flags

- D9: Informed by the EA model and awaits Product Marketing expert review; attribution, signal latency, and the boundary with demand generation need particular attention.
- D5–D8: The upper levels await validation against larger organizations than the one their first drafts came from.

## D1. Market discovery & definition

*The capability to identify, size, and validate market opportunities — including TAM/SAM/SOM, market trends, and opportunity framing — through a continuous, AI-assisted discovery loop that supplies a shared intelligence foundation for requirements, prioritization, and portfolio investment decisions.*

**Status:** Inherited · by reference

**Basis:** STD-SHARED-INTELLIGENCE v1.2 · 2026-10-16 (SDLC release v1.4.1), github.com/superdtf-0882/ai-native-sdlc-maturity-model/shared_intelligence_layer.md, by reference. Levels, transitions, verifications, and sustainment are rendered here from the standard at its tag and are edited only there

### A — Nascent

Discovery happens periodically, such as during planning cycles, and is manually synthesized. AI use is inconsistent and left to individual researcher preference, with no shared method.

**Transition from A to B**

Establish a consistent AI-assisted method for market-opportunity analysis. Define the minimum outputs required — including TAM/SAM/SOM, trend analysis, and opportunity framing — and use the same method for every discovery cycle, not only when someone requests it.

**Verification:** Two consecutive discovery cycles produce the same minimum output set — TAM/SAM/SOM, trend analysis, opportunity framing — by the same AI-assisted method, and the next cycle's date is known before the current one closes.

### B — Modeled

Discovery runs on a defined cadence, such as quarterly, using a consistent AI-assisted method. It remains one function's exercise, typically Product's: outputs are produced for that function's own use and are not routinely shared with the others that need them, whether Product, Marketing, Sales, or Strategy.

**Transition from B to C**

Move from cadence-based to continuous discovery. Replace the periodic cycle with a standing feed of market signals and opportunity-model updates. The test is whether a material market shift between planning cycles is detected and surfaced without waiting for the next scheduled review.

**Verification:** A material market shift that occurs between planning cycles appears in the opportunity model before the next scheduled review, without anyone having requested a refresh.

### C — Continuous

Discovery becomes continuous rather than cadence-based. Market signals, trend analysis, and opportunity models refresh on an ongoing basis. The intelligence remains one function's, typically Product's, although other functions can request access to current findings.

**Transition from C to D**

Open the discovery model as a shared asset. Product, Marketing, Sales Enablement, and Strategy should draw directly from the same underlying market and opportunity intelligence rather than request a summary from the function that maintains it, and no function should keep a version of its own. Define which outputs each function consumes and establish the access path.

**Verification:** A participant from a function other than the maintaining one retrieves current market and opportunity intelligence from the shared model directly, without a request to the maintaining function and without it producing a summary, and no function holds a separately maintained opportunity model.

### D — Integral

The continuous discovery model is a shared asset. Product, Marketing, Sales Enablement, and Strategy draw directly from the same underlying market and opportunity model rather than commissioning separate research or maintaining a version of their own. Each function still produces its own context-specific rendering manually.

**Transition from D to E**

Converge D1, D2, and D3 as one shared intelligence layer and automate function-specific rendering. A material market update should propagate into the relevant Product, Marketing, Sales, and Strategy outputs without a separate commissioning or human reformulation step.

**Verification:** A material market update propagates into the Product, Marketing, Sales, and Strategy renderings from the one layer, each in its audience's form, without a separate commissioning or human reformulation step — and the renderings agree with one another.

### E — Telemetric

D1, D2, and D3 operate as one converged intelligence layer that automatically generates function-specific renderings — including Product roadmap and requirements input, Marketing messaging, Sales battlecards, and Strategy narratives — from the same continuously updated model, each adapted to its audience without separate commissioning.

**Vantage:** From D1's vantage, market signals, trend data, and opportunity models are the input lens this layer continuously refreshes.

**Sustainment:** Verify that automatic renderings accurately reflect the shared intelligence and that no consuming function is silently receiving stale or miscontextualized output. Audit D1–D3 convergence periodically to confirm that the layer is coherent, not merely synchronized.

## D2. Buyer & user persona development

*The capability to research, model, and maintain live representations of target users and buyers as a shared intelligence asset across Product, Marketing, Sales Enablement, and Engineering.*

**Status:** Inherited · by reference

**Basis:** STD-SHARED-INTELLIGENCE v1.2 · 2026-10-16 (SDLC release v1.4.1), github.com/superdtf-0882/ai-native-sdlc-maturity-model/shared_intelligence_layer.md, by reference. Levels, transitions, verifications, and sustainment are rendered here from the standard at its tag and are edited only there

### A — Nascent

Buyer and user personas exist as static documents created at a point in time, such as a launch or major redesign, and are rarely revisited, whichever function keeps them. AI use, where present, is inconsistent and left to individual preference.

**Transition from A to B**

Establish a shared persona structure, clear ownership, and a minimum update cadence for both buyer and user personas. Adopt a consistent AI-assisted research and synthesis method rather than relying on departmental or individual practice.

**Verification:** Buyer and user personas share one structure, name an owner, carry the date of their last refresh and the date of the next, and that refresh has happened at least once on schedule.

### B — Modeled

Buyer and user personas are refreshed on a defined cadence using a consistent AI-assisted research and synthesis method and selected research, usage, or feedback data. They remain one function's, typically Product's for user personas and Marketing's for buyer personas. Other functions receive periodic document renderings, or nothing, rather than consuming a shared, live persona-intelligence system directly.

**Transition from B to C**

Move from periodic persona maintenance and document distribution to a live shared intelligence system. Connect persona attributes to ongoing research, usage, and feedback signals; establish common underlying data and AI intelligence; and extend direct access across Product, Marketing, Sales, and Engineering.

**Verification:** A new research, usage, or feedback signal changes a persona attribute in the shared system without a document being reissued, and a Marketing, Sales, or Engineering participant sees the change at the source.

### C — Continuous

Buyer and user personas are maintained in a shared system with common update data and AI intelligence. Usage, research, and feedback signals refresh the representations on an ongoing basis. Access extends across Product, Marketing, Sales, and Engineering, although the intelligence remains one function's to maintain, typically Product's, and consuming functions still interpret it for their own uses.

**Transition from C to D**

Make personas a directly consumed shared asset rather than a document distributed by the maintaining function. Product, Marketing, and Sales Enablement should draw from the same persona intelligence for their work, with no separately maintained set in any of them, while Engineering receives relevant user context through the requirements management system.

**Verification:** Product, Marketing, and Sales Enablement work from the shared persona intelligence with no separately maintained persona in any of them, and Engineering reaches relevant user context from inside the requirements management system rather than from a distributed document.

### D — Integral

Personas are dynamic, continuously maintained shared assets fed by usage, research, and feedback data. Product, Marketing, and Sales Enablement consume them directly rather than commissioning or maintaining separate personas; each buyer and user persona is kept once, in the shared set. Engineering has connected access to relevant user context through the requirements management system. Each function still produces its own context-specific rendering manually.

**Transition from D to E**

Converge D1, D2, and D3 as one shared intelligence layer and automate function-specific persona rendering. A meaningful update to buyer or user intelligence should propagate into the relevant Product, Marketing, Sales, Engineering, and Strategy outputs without manual reformulation.

**Verification:** A meaningful change to buyer or user intelligence propagates into the Product, Marketing, Sales, Engineering, and Strategy renderings without manual reformulation, and each rendering remains faithful to the underlying persona.

### E — Telemetric

D1, D2, and D3 operate as one converged intelligence layer that automatically generates function-specific renderings — including Product roadmap and requirements input, Marketing messaging, Sales battlecards, and Strategy narratives — from the same continuously updated model, each adapted to its audience without separate commissioning.

**Vantage:** From D2's vantage, live representations of target users and buyers are the input lens this layer continuously refreshes, extending to Engineering through the requirements management system.

**Sustainment:** Verify that automatically generated persona renderings remain faithful to the underlying intelligence and have not drifted because of stale data, automation bias, or function-specific reinterpretation. Audit D1–D3 convergence periodically.

## D3. Positioning & competitive intelligence

*The capability to define product differentiation and maintain that positioning against a continuously monitored competitive landscape, actively informing roadmap, prioritization, pricing, go-to-market, and portfolio decisions while preserving a current strategic thesis about what the organization intends to do better than competitors.*

**Status:** Inherited · by reference

**Basis:** STD-SHARED-INTELLIGENCE v1.2 · 2026-10-16 (SDLC release v1.4.1), github.com/superdtf-0882/ai-native-sdlc-maturity-model/shared_intelligence_layer.md, by reference. Levels, transitions, verifications, and sustainment are rendered here from the standard at its tag and are edited only there

### A — Nascent

Positioning was defined at a point in time, such as product launch, and competitive awareness is ad hoc or anecdotal. AI use, where present, is inconsistent and left to individual preference, with no shared method.

**Transition from A to B**

Establish a consistent AI-assisted competitive-monitoring method on a defined cadence. Require a minimum output set — including the competitor landscape, messaging differentiation, and positioning thesis — rather than initiating analysis only when a visible competitive threat appears.

**Verification:** Competitive monitoring has run on its cadence at least twice, each run producing the minimum output set — competitor landscape, messaging differentiation, positioning thesis — and the latest run was not triggered by a competitive event.

### B — Modeled

Competitive monitoring runs on a defined cadence using a consistent AI-assisted method. It remains one function's exercise, typically Product's, Strategy's, or Marketing's: findings inform that function's own roadmap and positioning discussions but are not routinely packaged for the other functions that need them.

**Transition from B to C**

Move from cadence-based to continuous competitive monitoring. Replace scheduled scans with a standing feed of competitor, market, pricing, and messaging signals. Maintain the positioning and strategic differentiation thesis as living intelligence rather than a launch artifact.

**Verification:** A competitor's pricing, product, or messaging change appears in the competitive feed before anyone circulates it by hand, and the positioning thesis carries a revision dated after launch.

### C — Continuous

Competitive monitoring becomes continuous. A live feed of market, pricing, product, and messaging shifts updates on an ongoing basis. Positioning and the strategic differentiation thesis remain primarily owned by Product or Strategy, although other functions can request access to current intelligence.

**Transition from C to D**

Open the competitive model as a shared asset. Product, Marketing, and Sales Enablement should draw directly from the same underlying intelligence for roadmap input, messaging, and battlecards, with no separately maintained competitive model in any of them. Maintain the strategic differentiation thesis — what the organization intends to do better than competitors — as an explicit, versioned, and auditable artifact.

**Verification:** Product, Marketing, and Sales Enablement build roadmap input, messaging, and battlecards from the shared competitive model rather than from commissioned or separately maintained research, and the strategic differentiation thesis can be read at a named version with its change history.

### D — Integral

The continuous competitive intelligence feed is a shared asset. Product, Marketing, and Sales Enablement draw directly from the same underlying competitive model rather than commissioning separate research or maintaining a feed of their own. The organization's positioning and strategic differentiation thesis are explicitly maintained and versioned as living artifacts. Each function still produces its own context-specific rendering manually.

**Transition from D to E**

Converge D1, D2, and D3 as one shared intelligence layer and automate function-specific rendering. Competitive, positioning, and differentiation changes should propagate into Product, Marketing, Sales, Pricing, and Strategy outputs without a separate commissioning or human reformulation step.

**Verification:** A competitive, positioning, or differentiation change propagates into Product, Marketing, Sales, Pricing, and Strategy renderings without a separate commissioning or reformulation step, and the thesis the renderings express is the one at the current version.

### E — Telemetric

D1, D2, and D3 operate as one converged intelligence layer that automatically generates function-specific renderings — including Product roadmap and requirements input, Marketing messaging, Sales battlecards, and Strategy narratives — from the same continuously updated model, each adapted to its audience without separate commissioning.

**Vantage:** From D3's vantage, positioning, the live competitive landscape, and the organization's strategic differentiation thesis are the input lens this layer continuously refreshes.

**Sustainment:** Verify that automatic competitive and positioning renderings accurately reflect the underlying intelligence and that the strategic differentiation thesis has not silently drifted through automation or local reinterpretation. Audit D1–D3 convergence periodically.

## D4. Knowledge corpus & standing

*The capability to maintain Product Marketing knowledge as a governed, machine-readable corpus, preserving provenance, source authority, and current standing, and providing authoritative context and derived renderings to humans and AI.*

**Status:** Native

**Basis:** Adapted from EA D2 and D6

### A — Nascent

Product and market knowledge is scattered across documents and individual AI contexts. AI-assisted drafting relies on manually supplied context, with inconsistent distinctions between evidence, drafts, issued guidance, and superseded material.

**Transition from A to B**

Name the kinds of artifact Product Marketing knowledge comes in — positioning, the messaging framework, claims and proof, enablement materials, release and adoption communication — and give each artifact a named owner, a version, its provenance, and a standing: draft, issued, or superseded. Put them in one governed place and make issuance an explicit act, so that being in the corpus and being in force are two different facts. Use a consistent AI-assisted method for retrieval and checking against the corpus.

**Verification:** A reader can pick any artifact in the corpus and establish, without asking its author, its owner, its version, where it came from, and whether it is in force; and the corpus holds at least one artifact that is in it and not in force, with the difference visible on the artifact.

### B — Modeled

A structured, governed corpus has defined artifact types, owners, provenance, versions, and standing. Product Marketing maintains the documents by hand; AI assists with retrieval and checking by a consistent method. Corpus membership alone does not place an artifact in force.

**Transition from B to C**

Convert the corpus from maintained documents to machine-readable records with fields a tool can check. Charter agents to maintain the records under written constraints, with a human validating meaning on material changes. Generate the human-facing views from the records instead of maintaining them separately, and make current guidance and its standing available on the surfaces where authors write.

**Verification:** A change made to one record appears in every generated view without any view being edited; an author drafting an asset is shown the standing of the guidance they are using at the moment of writing; and the record of the change names the agent that made it, the constraint it ran under, and the human who validated it.

### C — Continuous

The corpus is maintained as machine-readable knowledge by chartered agents under defined constraints, with human semantic validation. Human-facing views are generated from it rather than separately maintained. Current guidance and its standing are available at authorship; changes preserve provenance and issuance status.

**Transition from C to D**

Open the corpus as the operating substrate for Sales, partners, and their assistants: direct read of authoritative records, not distributed copies. Make every rendering carry the record and standing it was generated from, and build impact identification so that a change to a record lists the renderings it affects.

**Verification:** A Sales or partner assistant answers a product question from the corpus directly and its answer names the record and standing it drew on; and when that record changes, the list of affected renderings is produced before anyone asks for it.

### D — Integral

The corpus is a shared operating substrate for Product Marketing, Sales, partners, and their assistants. They consume authoritative knowledge directly in daily work. Changes identify affected renderings, and every rendering names the record and standing it was generated from, so a reader can establish whether what they hold is current and in force.

**Transition from D to E**

Instrument use: which records are consulted and by whom, which renderings are read, where a record is consulted past its standing, and where a rendering has drifted from its source. Let agents propose corpus corrections from that evidence and route them to authorized humans. Keep what readers did and what the corpus says they should do as two separate records.

**Verification:** A corpus correction is traceable to the usage evidence that prompted it, was proposed by an agent and authorized by a named human, and propagated to its affected renderings; and for any record, a reader can see what was consulted beside what was authoritative, without the two having been merged.

### E — Telemetric

Usage and execution evidence continuously reveal stale knowledge, records consulted past their standing, and renderings that have drifted from their source. Agents propose corpus corrections on that evidence; authorized changes propagate to affected renderings. Observed use and authoritative intent remain distinguishable.

**Sustainment:** Keep testing whether corpus corrections reach the renderings people and agents actually consult, including those produced before a source changed standing. Compare consultation records with the authoritative record without treating frequent use as authority. Retain the evidence, proposal, authorization, and propagation record for each correction. Use a controlled standing change where necessary to expose missed dependencies; repair the corpus or rendering path when a stale rendering remains usable as current guidance.

## D5. Messaging, content & proof

*The capability to translate product direction, positioning, and audience intelligence into coherent narratives, messaging, content, and substantiated claims.*

**Status:** Native

**Basis:** Native · from practitioner contribution (see the README's acknowledgments)

### A — Nascent

Messaging and content are written asset by asset. Claims are reused from memory or earlier decks, and their wording drifts each time they are paraphrased; internal terms reach the market in whatever wording each author chooses. AI drafting relies on whatever context and proof the author pastes in, so an unsupported or outdated claim can enter any asset without anyone noticing.

**Transition from A to B**

Write the messaging framework — narrative, audience messages, approved proof points — under a named Product Marketing owner. Give every material claim a named evidence source, approved wording, and the limits on how it may be used, and add the approved market-facing wording, and the wording to avoid, for each internal term the shared vocabulary (D10) marks for translation. Adopt a consistent AI-assisted drafting method that takes the framework as context, and review every draft against it.

**Verification:** For any material claim in a current asset, a reader can find its approved wording and evidence source in the framework without asking the author; and two assets produced by different authors in the same period state the same claim in the same approved wording.

### B — Modeled

A messaging framework, owned by Product Marketing, sets the narrative, audience messages, and approved proof points. Each material claim has a named evidence source, approved wording, and stated limits on how it may be used, and the framework carries the approved market-facing wording, and the wording to avoid, for each internal term the shared vocabulary (D10) marks for translation. AI-assisted drafting follows a consistent method with the framework as context; review against the framework is manual.

**Transition from B to C**

Turn the framework into structured claim records — claim, approved wording, evidence source, scope, review date — and bind them to the authoring surface, so that AI drafts from the records and checks drafts against them, flagging unsupported, out-of-scope, or expired claims and unapproved wording before a human reviews. Make the practice standing rather than periodic and available at authorship, alongside the shared vocabulary (D10) challenge. The checks may use one authoring surface or separate surfaces; both draw on the relevant governed records.

**Verification:** A draft containing a claim whose evidence has expired, or wording that is not approved, is flagged at authorship with the record cited, before any reviewer sees it; and every claim record carries a review date, with none past due and unflagged.

### C — Continuous

Messages, claims, proof, and market-facing wording are maintained continuously as structured records: claim, approved wording, evidence source, scope, and review date. AI drafts and checks content against them at authorship, alongside the shared vocabulary (D10) challenge, flagging unsupported, out-of-scope, or expired claims and unapproved wording before review. The practice is always on but remains owned and operated by Product Marketing.

**Transition from C to D**

Open the claim records to the go-to-market functions, Sales, Customer Success, partners, and their assistants. Stamp every asset with the provenance of the claims it uses and the collapse record for the terms it translated, and build the dependency so that a change to a claim, its evidence, or its approved wording lists every asset that depends on it.

**Verification:** An asset produced outside Product Marketing carries claim provenance that resolves to the records; a reader checks any claim's substantiation from the asset without asking Product Marketing; and when one claim's evidence is withdrawn, the list of dependent assets is produced and each is updated or withdrawn on record.

### D — Integral

The framework and claim records are load-bearing beyond Product Marketing. The go-to-market functions, Demand Generation among them, together with Sales, Customer Success, partners, and their assistants produce audience-specific content from them. Every asset carries the provenance of the claims it uses and the collapse record the shared vocabulary (D10) requires for the internal terms it translated, so a later reader can recover what a market-facing word meant inside. A change to a claim, its evidence, or an approved wording identifies every asset that depends on it, and a reader can check any claim's substantiation without asking Product Marketing.

**Transition from D to E**

Connect market-response learning (D9) to the claim records: content performance, claim challenges, and buyer misreads of market-facing wording. Let agents propose message revisions and proof retirements, route them to authorized approval before they propagate, and measure the response after each change. Keep resonance and validity as two separate judgments.

**Verification:** An observed case or a recorded controlled end-to-end exercise shows that a still-performing claim is withdrawn when its evidence weakens, with the record showing the evidence change, the agent's proposal, the approver, and propagation to dependent assets. In normal operation, a message revision is traceable to the response evidence that prompted it and to the measured response after it.

### E — Telemetric

Content performance, claim challenges, and buyer misreads from market-response learning (D9) flow back continuously. Agents propose revisions to messages, proof, and market-facing wording, retire proof that has aged or lost support, and route changes for authorized approval before they propagate to affected assets; a misread that indicts a term rather than a wording routes to the shared vocabulary (D10). Resonance and validity are kept distinct: a claim that performs well but whose evidence has weakened is withdrawn, not kept.

**Sustainment:** Keep testing proof validity independently of message performance. Sample revisions and retirements through their evidence, authorization, and dependent assets, including an effective claim whose support has weakened; a controlled demonstration can establish that path without waiting for such a case. Preserve before-and-after response evidence without letting improved resonance justify unsupported wording. When repeated buyer misreads concern a term's meaning rather than its expression, retain the referral to D10 and its disposition.

## D6. Sales & partner enablement

*The capability to equip sales and partners with current product knowledge, proof, objection handling, and audience-specific materials for commercial conversations.*

**Status:** Native

**Basis:** Native · from practitioner contribution (see the README's acknowledgments)

### A — Nascent

Enablement is event-driven. Decks, battlecards, and objection handling are sent at launch or on request, and reps and partners keep personal copies that go stale. Field AI use is individual: reps prompt their own assistants with whatever materials they happen to have.

**Transition from A to B**

Define the enablement program: a standard kit per segment and product, derived from the messaging framework, refreshed on a cadence, and distributed from one library that Product Marketing maintains. Produce and summarize the materials with a consistent AI-assisted method that takes the framework as context.

**Verification:** The library holds one current kit per segment and product, each with the date of its last refresh and the next; the library's access record shows the current kit retrieved by reps and partners in the period; and the kit's claims match the framework's approved wording.

### B — Modeled

A defined enablement program provides a standard kit per segment and product, refreshed on a cadence from the messaging framework. Materials are produced and summarized with a consistent AI-assisted method that takes the framework as context. Distribution runs through a known library maintained by Product Marketing; whether the field actually uses the kit is not measured.

**Transition from B to C**

Replace the cadence with continuous maintenance from the claim records (D5) and the competitive feed (D3). Withdraw superseded versions from circulation rather than leaving them beside current ones. Stand up a governed assistant that answers reps and partners from current materials and names its source, and begin measuring what the field uses.

**Verification:** A change to a claim or a competitor's move appears in the enablement materials before the next scheduled refresh would have; a search for the superseded version returns the current one or nothing; and a rep's question to the assistant is answered from current material with its source named.

### C — Continuous

Enablement materials are maintained continuously from current messaging (D5) and competitive intelligence (D3). Superseded versions are withdrawn from circulation rather than left beside current ones. Reps and partners can ask a governed assistant for current answers, but Product Marketing still curates and pushes the content.

**Transition from C to D**

Put guidance into the flow of deals: CRM records, call preparation, and partner portals surface stage- and account-specific content selected from the corpus, and field and partner assistants draw from that same source. Retire local copies.

**Verification:** A rep preparing a call sees the guidance for that stage and account inside the CRM record without going to the library; a partner assistant and a field assistant give the same answer with the same source; and a deal-preparation work record shows the shared guidance actually used, its source, and the preparation or decision that depended on it.

### D — Integral

Enablement reaches the moment of need. CRM records, call preparation, and partner portals surface stage- and account-specific guidance from the shared corpus, and field and partner assistants draw from the same source rather than local copies. What surfaces is current content selected by deal context, not a rendering generated for it; reps and partners still shape the conversation. Sales leadership and partner management depend on it; removing it would break how deals are worked.

**Transition from D to E**

Feed field and deal evidence back continuously — what was used, which objections arose, where conversations departed from guidance, why deals were won or lost. Let agents detect enablement gaps before reps report them and propose talk tracks and objection handling for authorized approval, and measure the effect of each change on later conversations.

**Verification:** An enablement update is traceable to deal evidence that no rep had reported — an objection pattern, a loss reason — was proposed by an agent and approved by a named human, and the record shows the objection's frequency in conversations before and after the change.

### E — Telemetric

Field and deal evidence (what was used, which objections arose, where conversations departed from guidance, why deals were won or lost) feeds back continuously. Agents detect enablement gaps before reps report them and propose updated talk tracks and objection handling for authorized approval, and the effect of each change on later conversations is measured.

**Sustainment:** Keep comparing agent-detected enablement gaps with later field and partner conversations. Trace revised guidance from deal evidence through authorization to what the field actually received, and retain the measured response, including changes that did not help. Sample different deal contexts so a well-observed segment does not conceal stale guidance elsewhere. When conversation evidence no longer reaches the correction loop, restore that path before treating the absence of reported gaps as evidence of adequacy.

## D7. Launch communication & readiness

*The capability to coordinate market introduction and field readiness through consistent launch narratives, assets, and communication aligned with product release intent.*

**Status:** Native

**Basis:** Native · from practitioner contribution (see the README's acknowledgments)

### A — Nascent

Each launch is assembled from scratch, often under deadline. Readiness is unknown until after the announcement, launch assets are produced in a scramble, and the connection to what Product Management intended to release is loose. AI use is individual and ad hoc.

**Transition from A to B**

Define launch tiers and a checklist per tier. Derive each launch brief from Product Management's release intent, produce the launch kit from the brief with a consistent AI-assisted method, and hold a go or no-go at which Product Marketing judges readiness against the checklist.

**Verification:** The last two launches each have a tier, a brief that names the release intent it came from, and a go or no-go record with the checklist items marked; and the launch narrative's claims match the brief.

### B — Modeled

Launches follow defined tiers and a checklist. A launch brief is derived from Product Management's release intent, the launch kit is produced from the brief with a consistent AI-assisted method, and Product Marketing judges readiness manually at a go or no-go decision.

**Transition from B to C**

Track readiness continuously against release state rather than only at go or no-go. Generate launch materials from the corpus and the messaging framework, and surface scope changes and slips to Product Marketing as they happen.

**Verification:** A scope change or slip made between the brief and the launch appears in the readiness view before the go or no-go meeting, without Product Management having to tell Product Marketing; and a launch asset regenerated after the change reflects it without hand editing.

### C — Continuous

Readiness is tracked continuously against release state, not only at go or no-go. Launch materials are generated from the shared corpus and messaging framework, and scope changes or slips surface to Product Marketing as they happen. Launch remains a Product Marketing-run practice.

**Transition from C to D**

Make one shared readiness record that Product Management, Engineering, Sales, Customer Success, and Support each contribute to and read, and have Product Management's release decision read that record rather than a readiness claim. On a scope change, update the record and the affected launch assets together.

**Verification:** Product Management's release decision cites the readiness record; each named function has an entry in it that the function wrote; and a scope change updates the record and the affected launch assets in the same act, with no function holding a readiness claim outside it.

### D — Integral

Launch readiness is load-bearing across Product Management, Engineering, Sales, Customer Success, and Support. Each function contributes to and reads one shared readiness record, and Product Management's release decision reads that record rather than a readiness claim. A change in scope updates the record and the affected launch assets together.

**Transition from D to E**

Tie market introduction to actual availability through staged rollout, so communication follows what each segment can use. Let agents monitor early response and propose adjustments for authorized approval, and measure launch effectiveness against the intent stated in the brief.

**Verification:** In a staged rollout, a segment that has not received the capability has not received the announcement; an adjustment to launch communication is traceable to early response evidence, an agent's proposal, and an approver; and launch effectiveness is reported against the brief's intent rather than against activity.

### E — Telemetric

Market introduction follows actual availability. As capabilities reach customers through staged rollout, communication follows what each segment can actually use. Agents monitor early response and propose adjustments for authorized approval, and launch effectiveness is measured against the intent in the launch brief.

**Sustainment:** Keep checking communication against actual availability by segment as rollout conditions change. Trace early response through an agent's proposed adjustment, authorized action, and subsequent results against the launch brief's intent. Retain cases where an adjustment did not improve the result. Use a controlled availability change when necessary to test whether affected communication follows it; an unchanged launch plan or a completed asset checklist cannot establish that the loop still operates.

## D8. Customer adoption communication

*The capability to translate product capabilities and changes into customer education, release communication, and guidance that supports adoption.*

**Status:** Native

**Basis:** Native · from practitioner contribution (see the README's acknowledgments)

### A — Nascent

Customer communication about product change is sporadic. Release notes appear when someone writes them, education materials are produced on request, and customers often learn about changes by running into them. AI use is individual.

**Transition from A to B**

Define the release communication cadence and the formats per customer role. Draft from release records with a consistent AI-assisted method. Product Marketing owns the cadence and formats; Product Marketing and Customer Success produce and send.

**Verification:** Each release in the last cadence has a communication in each role format, dated against the release record it was drafted from; and a customer-facing release note's content traces to that record rather than to memory.

### B — Modeled

Release communication and customer education follow a defined cadence and format, segmented by role. AI drafts from release records using a consistent method. Product Marketing owns the cadence and format; Product Marketing and Customer Success produce and send materials manually.

**Transition from B to C**

Maintain adoption materials continuously from release and corpus records so that guidance stays current with the product, and make current guidance retrievable on demand by customers and Customer Success.

**Verification:** Guidance retrieved for a capability matches the shipped behavior at the time of retrieval, including a change shipped since the last cadenced communication; and a Customer Success participant or a customer retrieves it without asking Product Marketing.

### C — Continuous

Adoption materials are maintained continuously from release and corpus records, so guidance stays current with the product. Customers and Customer Success can retrieve current guidance on demand. Communication is still broadcast by segment rather than shaped by what each customer actually uses.

**Transition from C to D**

Shape communication by adoption state: connect usage signals to targeting, have Customer Success, Support, and in-product surfaces draw from one source, and reach accounts and roles according to what they have and have not adopted.

**Verification:** A user who owns a capability and has not activated it receives relevant activation guidance, and an already activated user is excluded from that activation targeting; Customer Success, Support, and in-product surfaces give consistent guidance for the same context from the same source; and a renewal work record identifies the adoption state and the shared guidance actually used in the renewal work.

### D — Integral

Adoption communication is shaped by usage. Customer Success, Support, and in-product surfaces draw from the same source, and guidance reaches accounts and roles according to their actual adoption state, such as users who have not yet activated a capability they own. Targeting selects current guidance by adoption state; whether a communication changed adoption is not yet measured. Customer Success depends on it to drive renewal and expansion.

**Transition from D to E**

Close the loop: measure whether a communication changed adoption, let agents propose targeted guidance and send it within delegated authority — which requires D11 at D first — classify each gap as communication or product, and route product barriers to Product Management through market-response learning (D9).

**Verification:** A targeted communication's effect on activation is reported for the audience that received it against one that did not; an agent-sent communication cites the delegation it acted under; and a barrier classified as product appears in D9's routing to Product Management with the adoption evidence attached.

### E — Telemetric

Adoption signals and communication form a two-way loop. Agents propose targeted guidance and, within delegated authority (per D11-D), send it, then measure whether it changed adoption. Communication gaps are distinguished from product gaps, and product barriers route to Product Management through market-response learning (D9).

**Sustainment:** Keep checking that targeting follows current adoption state and that agents remain within the delegation governing each send. Retain comparisons of communication effect, including no improvement, and revisit gap classifications when later evidence contradicts them. Trace product barriers through D9 to Product Management and preserve their disposition separately from communication corrections. Use controlled changes in adoption state or delegated authority where needed to expose guidance that continues after its basis or permission has changed.

## D9. Market-response learning

*The capability to interpret message resonance, objections, competitive response, and adoption barriers, and return evidence to shared intelligence, Product Management, and Product Marketing work.*

**Status:** Native

**Basis:** Adapted from EA D3 and D9; aligned with PDLC D11 and the shared intelligence layer's feedback interfaces

### A — Nascent

Market response is interpreted through anecdotes, occasional reports, and individual AI-assisted summaries. Findings are rarely connected to the messages, audience assumptions, or materials that produced them.

**Transition from A to B**

Establish an AI-assisted market-response review on a cadence, owned by Product Marketing. Link each piece of evidence to the audience, message, claim, or asset it concerns, route findings by hand, and keep observations distinct from authorized changes to positioning or messaging.

**Verification:** The last two reviews ran on schedule; each finding in them names the message, claim, asset, or audience it concerns; and a finding that led to a positioning or messaging change is recorded separately from the change and its authorization.

### B — Modeled

A defined AI-assisted method, owned by Product Marketing, reviews market response on a scheduled cadence. Evidence is linked to the relevant audience, message, claim, or asset; humans assemble and route findings. Research observations remain distinct from authorized changes to positioning or messaging.

**Transition from B to C**

Gather and interpret response evidence continuously between reviews — from channels, sales calls, support, and win-loss — keep findings and anomalies linked to their context and available at any time, and use them in Product Marketing's own work.

**Verification:** A response anomaly — a claim challenged repeatedly, a message underperforming — is visible in the findings before the next scheduled review, with its context linked; and Product Marketing's own work product from that period cites it.

### C — Continuous

A standing AI-assisted practice gathers and interprets response evidence between scheduled reviews. Findings and anomalies remain linked to their context and are available continuously. Product Marketing uses them in its own work, but cross-functional routing and correction remain manual.

**Transition from C to D**

Route evidence to the corpus records and shared intelligence it concerns, with an accountable correction path across Product Marketing, Sales, Customer Success, and Product Management. Examine recurring friction for defects in assumptions, messages, or interfaces before attributing it to execution.

**Verification:** A finding that concerns a claim record, a persona, or the positioning thesis reaches that record's owner by a path the record shows, with a named accountable party and a disposition; and a recurring friction item carries a recorded examination of the assumption or message behind it before any finding about execution.

### D — Integral

Market-response learning is load-bearing across Product Marketing, Sales, Customer Success, and Product Management. Evidence is routed to affected corpus records and shared intelligence, with accountable correction paths. Recurring friction is examined for defects in assumptions, messages, or interfaces rather than attributed automatically to execution.

**Transition from D to E**

Connect response evidence to authorized adjustments and measure the response to them close to real time where signals permit. Let agents propose corrections to messages, audience assumptions, or underlying models and route changes beyond Product Marketing authority to their owners. Test whether corrections improved response, and keep association and supported causal evidence distinct.

**Verification:** An adjustment made on response evidence has a measured response after it, compared with a baseline or a control, and the record states whether the evidence supports causation or only association; and an agent's proposal that exceeded Product Marketing authority reached its owner with the evidence attached.

### E — Telemetric

A continuous two-way loop connects market-response evidence to authorized adjustments and measures the response to those adjustments close to real time where signals permit. Agents propose corrections to messages, audience assumptions, or underlying models and route changes beyond Product Marketing authority to their owners. The loop tests whether corrections improve response and distinguishes observed association from supported causal evidence.

**Sustainment:** Keep tracing response evidence through an authorized adjustment and the subsequent response, including unchanged or adverse results. Revisit causal judgments when audience, channel, or market conditions change, and preserve the basis for attribution rather than treating sequence as proof. Check correction paths beyond Product Marketing for unresolved referrals. Judge loop operation against when signals can actually arrive; a quiet market interval does not establish failure, and a stream of agent proposals does not establish learning.

## D10. Shared language

*The capability to hold one governed vocabulary for the enterprise's working language — not the architecture's formal ontology — and operate it in both directions: resolving a term inward to its precise sense, and translating it outward with a record of what was collapsed.*

**Status:** Inherited · by reference

**Basis:** STD-EA-MM D10 Shared language (Vocabula) at v1.1.2 (model text v1.1.1), github.com/superdtf-0882/ai-native-ea-maturity-model, by reference. Levels, transitions, verifications, and sustainment are rendered here from the source at its tag and are edited only there; the vantage paragraph at E is this model's

### A — Nascent

Terms are used without shared referents and nothing surfaces the divergence. The same word means different things in different functions, and the difference is discovered in rework — a launch built for one population, a contract written about another. Either no glossary exists, or one exists and nobody can say where. Outward-facing language is written per document, in whatever voice the author has.

**Transition from A to B**

Enumerate the terms that actually collide, each with its distinct senses and the function that owns each sense. **The test of inclusion is collision in practice, not importance in the abstract** — a term nobody disputes does not need a row. Each sense states what it covers and what it excludes, because a definition without an exclusion cannot settle an argument.

**Verification:** A reader can establish, without asking the author, that the enterprise distinguishes *customer*, *buyer* and *user*, and what each one excludes.

### B — Modeled

A governed vocabulary exists. Terms have senses, senses have owners, and the document is findable. **Nothing consults it.** The definitions are correct and inert — read when someone is already suspicious, which is the case where they were least needed. Outward translation remains a per-author judgment. **An organization at B typically believes it is higher, because the artifact exists and is not wrong.**

**Transition from B to C**

Bind the vocabulary to the surfaces where writing happens — the editor, the ticket, the document, the agent's context — so the definition **arrives at the moment of authorship** instead of waiting to be looked up. An agent meeting an ambiguous term challenges it in one interaction, **cites the vocabulary as the source of the challenge**, and makes the answer cheap to give.

**Verification:** An author writing an ambiguous term is challenged as they write it; the challenge names the vocabulary it came from; and answering costs one click rather than a sentence.

### C — Continuous

The vocabulary is active at the point of authorship. Ambiguity is surfaced rather than discovered downstream, and the challenge cites its source so the author can judge whether to trust it. **The author can answer *loose sense, deliberately* and proceed.** Outward-facing language is still handled term by term: nothing distinguishes a term that must be translated for an external reader from one that may cross unchanged.

**Transition from C to D**

**Two moves, one capability.** *Inward:* give the agent enough governed context — function, audience, document type — that a term whose sense the context settles is **resolved rather than queried**, with the agent stating which sense it used. **Asking is correct for genuine ambiguity and is a tax everywhere else.** *Outward:* at the boundary the operation inverts. An internal term leaving for an external reader is not disambiguated, it is **translated**, and the translation **records what it collapsed**.

**Verification:** For an internal document in a known function, the agent resolves without asking and names the sense it used. For an outbound document, the same term raises the reverse challenge — *this word carries three senses inside and one outside; is the collapse intended?* — and the answer is recorded with the document.

### D — Integral

One vocabulary, two governed operations. Inside, terms resolve from context and the agent states its reading, so precision is affordable and traversal is deep — a term reaches its sense, its siblings and its owner. At the boundary, translation is a controlled act whose collapses are recorded with the document that made them, so a later reader of the contract and a later reader of the press release can each establish what was meant. **The distinction survives the crossing in both directions.**

**Transition from D to E**

Instrument both operations: where terms are queried, where they resolve, where authors mark a sense deliberately loose, and where an outbound translation is overridden. **Read the results as evidence about the MODEL rather than about the authors.** A distinction repeatedly waved away is one the enterprise does not hold.

**Verification:** A term is retired, merged or re-scoped on the evidence of its own usage record, and the change is traceable to the measurements that prompted it.

### E — Telemetric

The vocabulary is a measured instrument. The enterprise can see which distinctions its people operate and which they route around, and corrects the model on that evidence rather than on advocacy. **Terms are retired and merged as readily as they are added.** The outward translation layer is tuned by what external readers actually misread, not by what internal authors assume they will.

**Vantage:** From Product Marketing's vantage, the external readers are buyers, the field, and partners; what they are given to read is D5's approved wording, and what they misread arrives through D9.

**Sustainment:** Reconcile the model against its own usage record on a standing cadence, retiring distinctions nobody operates. Both failure modes are live here: a term that matters only in rare documents reads as dead to a record that counts volume, and one retired on aggregate waivers takes the single function still using it.

## D11. Governance, delegation & authority

*The capability to define, delegate, enforce, and evaluate decision rights for Product Marketing work across human and AI actors, including permitted actions, covered surfaces, limits, and exceptions.*

**Status:** Native

**Basis:** Adapted from EA D7; governance improvement informed by EA D9

### A — Nascent

Human and AI actors operate on implicit or inconsistent decision rights. Drafting, approving, issuing, and publishing are not reliably distinguished; the systems, materials, and channels covered by a delegation are unstated.

**Transition from A to B**

Enumerate Product Marketing's consequential actions — issuing messaging, approving a claim, publishing an asset, sending to customers, committing partner materials — and for each name who may authorize it and who may perform it, over which surfaces (channels, systems, materials), and within what limits. Write the map, keep it under Product Marketing, and have AI-assisted work carry it as context by a consistent method.

**Verification:** For any consequential action taken in the period — a published asset, a sent communication — a reader can establish from the map who was entitled to authorize it and on which surface, and the record of the action names that person; and every delegation in the map states at least one surface and one limit, not only a name.

### B — Modeled

Decision rights and delegations are explicitly mapped to Product Marketing work, naming who may authorize or perform each consequential action, over which surfaces, and within which limits. The map is maintained by Product Marketing and enforcement remains manual. AI-assisted work operates under those documented responsibilities by a consistent method that takes the delegation map as its context.

**Transition from B to C**

Make the authority checks machine-verifiable: a check runs before release or execution and clears or blocks the action against the map. Test every boundary by attempting an action outside its delegation rather than reading its configuration, and keep human authority over issuance and consequential commitments explicit.

**Verification:** For each declared enforced boundary, an attempted action outside a delegation — by a test actor, human or agent — was blocked, and the block is on record with the boundary it hit; and an issued asset carries the identity of the human who issued it as established by the check, not by the asset's byline.

### C — Continuous

Machine-verifiable authority checks continuously clear or block consequential actions before release or execution. Each enforced boundary is tested by attempting an action outside its delegation rather than trusting configuration. Human authority over issuance and consequential commitments remains explicit.

**Transition from C to D**

Mediate consequential actions at runtime for both human-assisted and autonomous work. Make a change to authority change operational permissions, state the exclusions at each mediation point, and grant agents bounded delegated work that needs no new decision for each permitted action.

**Verification:** A change to a delegation in the map changes what an agent can do at its next attempt with no other configuration touched; an agent performed a permitted consequential action without a human decision for that instance, inside a stated delegation; and each mediation point's exclusions are readable where it runs; and an action outside a delegation is refused at runtime, with the refusal on record.

### D — Integral

The authority plane mediates consequential actions at runtime for both human-assisted and autonomous work. Changes to authority alter operational permissions; every mediation point states its exclusions. Agents can perform bounded delegated work without a new committee decision for each permitted action.

**Transition from D to E**

Instrument the plane: boundary hits, exceptions, friction, and controls that never fire, evaluated against a stated risk appetite. Let agents propose changes to delegation and enforcement on that evidence, with authorized humans endorsing consequential authority changes, and re-establish boundary reach whenever a tool or channel is added.

**Verification:** An observed case or a recorded controlled end-to-end exercise traces a delegation change to boundary evidence — a control that never fired, recurring exceptions — through an agent proposal and endorsement by a named human; the stated risk appetite is on record and the evaluation cites it. A tool or channel added during the period, or introduced in a controlled exercise when none was added, has its boundary reach re-established by recorded tests.

### E — Telemetric

Telemetry continuously evaluates boundary hits, exceptions, friction, and controls that never fire against stated risk appetite. Agents propose changes to delegation and enforcement on that evidence; authorized humans endorse consequential authority changes. Boundary reach is re-established as tools and channels change.

**Sustainment:** Keep testing the authority plane as delegations, actors, tools, and channels change. For each declared enforced boundary, retain an out-of-delegation attempt and its result; use controlled attempts rather than waiting for an unauthorized action. Trace changes to boundary evidence, stated risk appetite, and human endorsement, and check that revised permissions operate at the next attempt. Investigate controls that never fire without assuming either that they are unnecessary or that silence proves their reach.
